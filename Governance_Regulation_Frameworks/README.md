## What This Project Is

---

## What I Learned

### The Basics: Governance vs Regulation vs Compliance
- **Governance** → how the organisation sets direction, makes decisions, and ensures everyone follows the right rules
- **Regulation** → laws or standards enforced by external bodies — you don’t have a choice, you have to meet them
- **Compliance** → proving you’re actually doing what’s required

### Why It Matters
Good governance and regulation don’t just avoid fines — they:
- Build real stakeholder trust
- Improve overall security posture
- Align security spending with business goals
- Help make better, more informed decisions
- Can become a genuine competitive advantage

### Key Laws & Standards
| Regulation | Area | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **GDPR** | Data privacy | EU/UK personal data handling — strict rules, big penalties |
| **HIPAA** | Healthcare | Protecting patient health information (US) |
| **PCI-DSS** | Payments | Securing credit card processing |
| **ISO 27001** | Info security | International standard for managing security systematically |
| **NIST 800-53** | Controls | Catalogue of specific safeguards — Access Control, Incident Response, Risk Assessment & more |

### The GRC Framework — Three Core Parts
1. **Governance** → set the strategy, write the policies, define what success looks like
2. **Risk Management** → find what could go wrong, measure it, put protections in place
3. **Compliance** → follow the laws/standards, audit, report, prove you’re doing it right

### How to Build a Policy
It’s not just writing a document — the full process:
1. **Identify scope & purpose** → what are we covering and why?
2. **Research** → laws, standards, best practice
3. **Draft** → clear, actionable, aligned with business goals
4. **Review & approval** → experts, stakeholders, leadership sign off
5. **Implement & communicate** → train people, make sure they follow it
6. **Review & update** → regularly check it still fits threats and regulations

### Real-World Examples
- **Password Policy** → minimum length, complexity, storage rules, change frequency
- **Incident Response Procedure** → define incident types, assign roles, step-by-step actions, reporting, evidence handling
- **NIST 800-53 Controls** → specific control families: Access Control, Audit & Accountability, Incident Response, Risk Assessment

---

## What Stuck With Me Most
- Policy without implementation is useless — writing it is 10%, getting people to follow it is the other 90%
- Different industries follow different standards — finance, healthcare, government all have their own frameworks
- NIST 800-53 doesn't just say "be secure" — it gives specific, actionable safeguards for every part of an organisation
- Review and update never stops — threats change, laws change, policies must keep up
- GRC isn't separate from security — it defines what we protect, how we measure success, and what we report when things go wrong
